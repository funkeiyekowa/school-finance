Chapter 01

How to use this manual

This manual has one job: get you to the correct answer in under a minute, whether you're a bursar closing the day's receipts or a class teacher marking the register. Read this page once — it explains how everything else is organised.

Chapter 02

Getting started

Everyone's first ten minutes on the platform look the same, whether they end up running the whole school or recording a single fee. Here's how to get from a blank login screen to a working account.

Signing in for the first time

Open your school's app URL — for Grant Schools this is school-finance-navy.vercel.app. You'll land on the welcome screen with two tabs: Sign in and Register.

  • Already have an account? Use Sign in with your email and password, or Continue with Google.
  • Brand new here? Use Register. If you are the very first person from your school to ever register, you automatically become the school's Admin — everyone who joins afterwards needs a school code and your approval.

Registering with a school code

If your school is already set up on the platform, someone there — your admin or bursar — will give you a short code. You'll need it to register:

  1. Open the app URL and click Register.
  2. Enter your full name.
  3. Enter the school code you were given.
  4. Enter your email and choose a password.
  5. Submit — you'll see Waiting for approval.

Once your school admin approves you (Chapter 04), sign in and you're in.

Why a school code at all?

Without it, a new account has no way to say which school it belongs to — it would float free, visible to no one until someone manually sorted it out. The code pins your account to the right school the moment you register.

Continue with Google

You can also register or sign in with Continue with Google. After the Google step completes, first‑time users land on the same pending page and are asked for a school code if they don't already belong to one.

What "Waiting for approval" means

Registering doesn't grant access by itself — it puts your account in a pending state. A school admin has to approve you and assign your role before you can see any data. This is deliberate: it stops anyone who merely knows the school code from reading fee records. See Chapter 04 — Team management for how approvals work from the admin's side.

Belong to more than one school?

Some people — a bursar who consults for two schools, say — belong to multiple organisations. The name at the top of your sidebar shows which one is currently active, and you can switch between them at any time. Full details are in Chapter 04.

Chapter 03

Roles & permissions

Every person at your school holds exactly one role, and that role decides what they can see and do. Ten roles cover everything from full control of the school to a single parent portal.

The ten roles

Roles are scoped per school — someone can hold a different role at another school they also belong to, at the same time.

RoleAccess level
ownerFull control of your school — the highest role your school itself can grant.
adminEverything except billing.
bursarFinance operations — income, expenses, reports, and reconciliation.
accountantFinance access, read‑heavy — reporting and oversight rather than daily entry.
editorCan record and edit transactions, but not manage the team or settings.
staffGeneral access to core, non‑financial areas.
teacherTeaching modules — attendance, assessments, timetable, and the teacher‑facing portal.
parentParent portal only.
studentStudent portal only.
viewerRead‑only, for anyone who needs visibility without the ability to change anything.

How a role becomes a permission

  1. The platform looks at your membership role at your school.
  2. It matches that role to a role definition — a set of permissions for your school.
  3. That definition decides which sidebar items and features you can see.
  4. Owners and admins always receive full permissions regardless of the definition.
Choosing a role for a new hire

Match the role to what the person actually needs to touch. A new front‑desk hire who only takes fee payments is a cleaner fit for bursar or editor than admin — it's easier to widen access later than to explain what you've just removed.

Changing someone's role

A school admin changes roles from Team → find the user → the role dropdown. Step‑by‑step walkthrough in Chapter 04.

Chapter 04

Team management

The Team page is where your school controls who's in the building. Every join, approval, and role change for your school happens here — this chapter covers all of it in depth.

Requires School Admin or Owner

What you'll find on the Team page

Sidebar → Team. This is your school's roster: everyone with a membership at your school, their role, and their status. An amber banner at the top surfaces anyone waiting on you right now.

Active members

Everyone currently approved, with their role shown next to their name.

Pending approval

People who registered with your school code but haven't been let in yet.

Invite user

Your school's join code, with a copy button, for bringing new people in.

The join code system

Your school has its own short alphanumeric join code — something like AB3F9K — that identifies it during registration. It's how the platform knows which school a new signup belongs to.

Where to find it

Open Team → Invite User — your code is shown prominently, with a copy button.

How it's used

  1. You share the code with new staff: "Register at [app URL] and use code AB3F9K."
  2. They register and enter the code — the platform links their account to your school.
  3. They land on Waiting for approval.
  4. You approve them from the Team page (steps below) and they're in.
If the code leaks

Anyone with your code can start a registration, though they still need your approval to get in. If it's shared with people who shouldn't have it, go to Team → Invite User → Generate new code. The old code stops working immediately.

Approving a pending user

  1. Open Team in the sidebar.
  2. Pending users appear in an amber banner at the top of the page.
  3. Choose a role for them from the dropdown.
  4. Click Approve.

They can sign in immediately afterwards, with exactly the access their new role allows.

Changing an existing member's role

Open Team, find the person in the active members list, and use the role dropdown next to their name to select a new role. The change takes effect the next time they load a page — no need to have them sign out.

Switching between schools

If you belong to more than one school, you don't need separate logins. Switch context from wherever you are:

  1. Look at the top of the sidebar — it shows your current school's name.
  2. Click it. A dropdown lists every school you belong to.
  3. Choose a different school. The page reloads showing that school's data.
What's really happening

Switching isn't a cosmetic toggle. Nothing about other schools' data is ever loaded into a session that isn't currently active for them — you always see exactly one school at a time.

Chapter 05

Your dashboard

Sidebar → Dashboard is the first screen you see, and every figure on it is pulled live from your ledgers — nothing is cached, nothing is estimated.

Summary cards

CardWhat it tells you
Total IncomeSum of all receipts recorded, with a count of how many.
Total ExpensesSum of all vouchers recorded for the period.
Net PositionIncome less expenses — your running balance.
Outstanding FeesWhat's still owed across all students, plus how many entries are unreconciled.

Cash flow by month

This chart plots income against expenses over time, so you can see the shape of the term at a glance — a steep climb in the income line, for instance, tells you a wave of fee payments has come in, well before you'd spot it in a table of numbers.

Fee balances

A running list of students with unpaid amounts sits right on the dashboard, so you always know exactly who to follow up with. Click All students to see the complete list rather than just the handful shown.

Alerts banner

Anything needing your attention — like payment alerts nobody has reviewed yet — surfaces in a banner directly under the summary cards. Click Review to open them.

Outstanding Fees ≠ unreconciled

The dashboard's "Outstanding Fees" card mixes two different things — money students still owe, and entries nobody has reconciled yet. Treat them as two separate to‑do lists: one is a collections task, the other is a bookkeeping task. See Chapter 06.

On the day this page was written, Grant Schools' dashboard read: Total Income ₦253,400 from 5 receipts · Total Expenses ₦4,600 · Net Position ₦248,800 · Outstanding Fees ₦447,000 across 15 unreconciled entries — with an amber banner flagging 71 SMS payment alerts waiting for review. Your own numbers will differ day to day; the point of this page is what each card means, not what it happens to say right now.

Chapter 06

Finance operations

Every naira the school touches is logged as either a receipt coming in or a voucher going out. This chapter covers recording both, reconciling them against the bank, and reading the reports that come out the other end.

Recording income (receipts)

Sidebar → Income. Use this for fee payments, donations, and any other money coming in.

  • Every receipt gets an auto‑generated receipt number, unique to your school.
  • Link a receipt to a student to keep their fee balance accurate automatically.
  • Choose a payment method: Cash, Transfer, POS, or Bank Deposit.

Recording expenses (vouchers)

Sidebar → Expenses. Use this for everything the school spends.

  • Every expense gets a voucher number, and can be linked to a vendor.
  • Categorise it — Utilities, Salaries, Supplies, and others you define.
  • Larger or sensitive expenses can move through an approval workflow before they're final.

Reconciliation

Reconciliation is where your records meet the bank's records.

  1. Bring up your bank statement alongside the platform's transaction list.
  2. Match each statement line to a recorded transaction.
  3. Anything that doesn't match is flagged automatically — investigate before you close the period.
  4. Mark matched transactions as reconciled once you're confident they agree.

Reports

Pull these whenever you need a clean number for a meeting, a proprietor, or the board:

Income vs. expense summaries

Your net position over any period you choose.

Student balance reports

Who owes what, school‑wide or by class.

Category breakdowns

Where the money is actually going, by expense category.

Period comparisons

This term against last term, this year against last year.

Chapter 07

Student management

Students are the anchor for almost everything else in the platform — fees, attendance, assessments, and promotion all hang off the student record.

Student records

Sidebar → Students. Each record holds:

  • A unique student code — unique within your school.
  • Personal details and guardian information.
  • Class assignment and academic year.
  • Status: Active, Graduated, or Withdrawn.

Because fee balances (Chapter 06) are linked to student records, keeping this list accurate is what keeps your outstanding‑fees numbers trustworthy.

Promotion

Sidebar → Promotion. At the end of an academic year:

  1. Open the Promotion tool and review the class list.
  2. Batch‑promote students to their next class in one action, rather than editing each record individually.
  3. Update the academic year so new records and reports file under the correct term going forward.
Do this before the new term starts

Run promotion once the current year's records are finalised, and before you start recording fees for the new term — that way every new receipt attaches to the right class and year from day one.

Chapter 08

Attendance

A daily register for every class, feeding straight into the attendance patterns your teachers and admin team rely on.

Marking the register

Sidebar → Attendance. Available to anyone with the teacher role, plus school admins.

  1. Select a class and a date.
  2. Mark each student Present, Absent, or Late.
  3. Save the register — it's timestamped and attributed to whoever marked it.

Reviewing attendance history

Filter by student or by class to see attendance over a term rather than a single day — useful for spotting a pattern before it becomes a problem, and for anything a parent asks about at a meeting.

Mark it the same day

Attendance recorded well after the fact is harder to trust and harder to correct if a mistake creeps in. Build it into the start of each class period as a habit, rather than an end‑of‑week catch‑up.

Chapter 09

Timetable

Where each class, subject, and teacher sits across the week — the schedule every other module quietly assumes is correct.

Building a timetable

Sidebar → Timetable.

  1. Choose the academic term you're scheduling for (Chapter 07 covers academic years).
  2. Add periods for each class — subject, teacher, day, and time slot.
  3. Save. Clashes — the same teacher or room booked twice at once — are flagged so you can resolve them before publishing.

Sharing it

Once published, teachers see their own schedule under My Teaching (Chapter 15), and the timetable underlies which subjects show up when you mark attendance or enter assessment scores.

Chapter 10

Assessments

Continuous assessment, tests, and exam scores, entered against the class and subject they belong to.

Entering scores

Sidebar → Assessments.

  1. Select a class, subject, and assessment — a specific test, or the term's continuous assessment.
  2. Enter each student's score against the criteria you've set up.
  3. Save — scores roll up into that student's overall standing for the subject and term.

Where this shows up

Scores you enter here are what a teacher sees under My Results (Chapter 15) and, depending on your school's configuration, what feeds report cards and the parent/student portal.

Keep grading windows tight

Close a term's assessment entry once report cards are being prepared, so a late score doesn't silently change a result after cards have already gone out.

Chapter 11

CBT & online exams

Computer‑based tests students sit directly on the platform — built once, scheduled, and auto‑marked where the question type allows it.

Setting up an exam

Sidebar → CBT / Exams.

  1. Build a question set for a subject — multiple‑choice questions can be marked automatically; longer answers need manual review.
  2. Set the exam window — when it opens, and how long students have.
  3. Assign it to the class or classes sitting it.
  4. Share the exam with students; it appears under their own My Exams (Chapter 15) once the window opens.

During and after

Watch submissions come in as students finish. Once the window closes, auto‑marked scores are ready immediately; anything needing manual review, you mark before publishing final results.

Test the exam yourself first

Sit through your own question set as a preview before the real window opens — it's the fastest way to catch a wrong answer key or a badly worded question before a whole class hits it.

Chapter 12

Staff records

A directory of everyone who works at the school — separate from, but connected to, the login accounts covered in Chapter 04.

Staff records vs. logins

Team (Chapter 04) controls who can sign in and what they can do inside the platform. Staff is your HR record for that same person — and for anyone on payroll who may never need to log in at all, such as a groundskeeper or a driver.

Managing staff records

Sidebar → Staff.

  • Bio data and contact information.
  • Position and department.
  • Employment status and start date.
  • Documents you choose to attach, such as a contract or qualification.
Link where it matters

Where a staff member also has a login account, keeping their name identical in both places makes the two records far easier to match later, especially in reports.

Chapter 13

Inventory

What the school owns and what it's running low on — from furniture to exercise books.

Tracking stock

Sidebar → Inventory.

  1. Add an item with a name, category, and starting quantity.
  2. Log stock in when new supplies arrive, and stock out when items are issued or used.
  3. Set a reorder threshold so low stock is easy to spot before it becomes a shortage.

Why this matters for finance

Larger stock purchases are usually also recorded as an expense (Chapter 06) — Inventory tracks the physical item, the expense record tracks the money. Keeping both up to date is what lets you answer "how much did we spend on X, and where did it all go?" with one lookup instead of a search through paper.

Chapter 14

Announcements

One place to reach staff, parents, or the whole school — instead of a dozen separate phone calls.

Posting an announcement

Sidebar → Announcements.

  1. Write the announcement.
  2. Choose its audience — the whole school, a specific class, or staff only.
  3. Publish. It reaches recipients through whichever channel your school has enabled — in‑app, SMS, or both.
Keep the audience narrow on purpose

A message sent to "everyone" trains people to skim; a message sent to exactly the right class or team gets read. Narrow the audience whenever you can.

Chapter 15

My Teaching, Results & Exams

Three personalised views that show only what belongs to you — your classes, your results to manage, and your exams — rather than the whole school's data.

My Teaching

Sidebar → My Teaching, visible to anyone with the teacher role. Shows the classes and subjects assigned to you, with quick links into that class's attendance register and assessment entry — the same underlying data as Chapters 08 and 10, filtered to just your own timetable.

My Results

Sidebar → My Results. For a teacher, this is the scores you're responsible for entering or have already submitted. For a student or parent account, it's the results that belong to that student — nothing else.

My Exams

Sidebar → My Exams. Whatever CBT exams (Chapter 11) are currently assigned to you — to sit, if you're a student, or to invigilate and mark, if you're a teacher.

Same platform, different lens

These three pages don't hold separate data from the rest of the platform — they're the main Attendance, Assessments, and CBT records, automatically filtered to whoever is signed in. There's nothing extra to maintain here.

Chapter 16

Website studio

Your school can run its own public website from inside the platform — no separate builder, no separate login. This is where you build and publish it.

Accessing it

Sidebar → Website Studio. This only appears if the website module is enabled for your school — ask your school admin if you don't see it.

First‑time setup

  1. Choose a starting theme — five are available.
  2. The system creates a home page pre‑filled with starter sections.
  3. Edit the content, add your logo, and set your contact details.
  4. Click Publish when it's ready for the public.

Your public website URL

Before you buy a custom domain, your site is live at your-app.vercel.app/s/school-slug — for Grant Schools, that's school-finance-navy.vercel.app/s/grant-schools.

Section types you can add

Hero, About, Principal's Message, Why Choose Us, Values, Programmes, Facilities, Statistics, Achievements, Testimonials, Staff, Gallery, Video, News (dynamic), Events (dynamic), FAQ, Admissions CTA, Contact, and more — mix and match under Pages & Sections.

Chapter 18

Troubleshooting

Seven issues cover almost everything a school team runs into. Click a question to expand it — every one of these you can resolve yourself.

Cause: your school admin hasn't approved you yet — there's no automatic waiting period.

Fix: ask your school admin to open Team and click Approve next to your name.

Cause: they most likely registered with the wrong school code, so their account is linked elsewhere.

Fix: double‑check the code you shared against the one shown under Team → Invite User, and have them register again with the correct code if it doesn't match.

Cause: either it isn't enabled on your school's plan, or your role doesn't include it.

Fix: ask your school admin to check your role (Chapter 03), or contact your platform provider to have the module switched on for your school.

Cause: a receipt may have been recorded against the wrong student, entered twice, or entered with the wrong amount.

Fix: open the student's record and review their payment history line by line (Chapter 06), then correct or remove the erroneous entry.

Cause: the usual — a mistyped or forgotten password.

Fix: use "Forgot password" on the sign‑in screen, or Continue with Google if that's how you originally registered. If neither works, ask your school admin to check your account status under Team.

Cause: not a fault — just a routine offboarding step that's easy to forget.

Fix: go to Team, find them in the active members list, and change their status to remove access. Do this on their last day, not weeks later.

Cause: they're viewing a page loaded before the role change took effect.

Fix: refresh the page, or sign out and back in — role changes apply on the next page load.
